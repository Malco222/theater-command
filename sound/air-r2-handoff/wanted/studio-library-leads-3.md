# Studio-library replacements: leads, round three (24 Nov 2026)

A second search for the sources round two missed (Jumo 213, Limbo and other anti-submarine weapons, NASA and FAA crash tests, reef sinkings, torpedo ranges). Four searchers, each followed by a skeptic who tried to refute every lead against Brian's rules (real recordings only; nothing from films, series, games, newsreels or studio/effects libraries; CC0, public domain, CC BY or CC BY-NC). **None is heard yet.** Kept leads below; the fetch list is `studio-library-urls-3.txt` (only the ones a Mac can download: YouTube-only and navy.mil items are left out, as last time). The raw results are `studio-library-search-3-2026-11-24.json`. A new fetch needs Brian's yes first.

**Short version.** Still no free, real Jumo 211 or Ju 87, no real depth-charge thrower, Hedgehog or Limbo, no torpedo tube beyond round two's, and no aircraft breaking up in the air. What is new: more stand-ins of the kind round two found (a frigate's chaff launcher on an Australian War Memorial tape, US chaff and mortar B-roll, a National Park Service cannon), real NASA and FAA crash tests of whole airframes onto dirt, and more US Navy sinking-exercise videos.

### Spitfires and Bf-109 Flyby by Fight2FlyPhoto (Freesound 142902)
- **For:** `stuka_drone_loop`. **URL:** https://freesound.org/people/Fight2FlyPhoto/sounds/142902/
- **Licence:** unknown (page search snippet only; skeptic: licence unknown). The search snippet shows only the title and uploader, not the licence. It comes from the same uploader as the two Fight2FlyPhoto Bf 109 leads already found (142898, 242179), so it may carry the same licence. Check before use.
- **What it is:** The search title says it is a flyby of Spitfires and a Bf 109. The uploader's other Bf 109 items name a Daimler-Benz engine ('Bf-109 - Daimler-Benz Run-Up'), and a Cities and Memory re-post of 242179 describes their 109 as a restored Bf 109E-3 with a DB 601. That suggests this is a DB 601 inverted V12 in flight, but the snippet does not confirm it. Date, place, mic setup and length are not visible.
- **Fit:** An inverted-V12 German engine in flight. The DB 601 is close in size and layout to the Jumo 211 (both about 34-35 L inverted V12s). It is the airborne counterpart to the ground runs already found.
- **Concerns:** (1) Merlin-engined Spitfires are mixed in, so a clean 109-only section may be short. (2) It is a flyby rather than a steady drone, and it is one aircraft, not a formation. (3) The licence is not verified from here.

The DB 601 / Bf 109E-3 detail comes from a different upload (242179), and many airshow '109s' that fly with Spitfires are Merlin-powered Spanish Buchons, which would make this useless as a Jumo substitute. (3) Spitfire Merlins are mixed in. (4) It is a single flyby, not a sustained formation drone. Drop it unless the page names a Daimler-Benz engine and a qualifying licence.

## Depth-charge thrower (depth_charge_thrower)

### HMAS Adelaide, Adelaide class guided missile Frigate - operational sounds (S04691)
- **For:** `depth_charge_thrower`. **URL:** https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C1240475
- **Licence:** CC BY-NC (page page read; skeptic: licence yes). The page says: 'Item copyright: © Australian War Memorial'. It is licensed under CC BY-NC 3.0 Australia. Copying provisions: 'Only personal, non-commercial, research and study use permitted.'
- **What it is:** A Defence Public Relations (DPR) sound-effect tape recorded aboard the frigate HMAS Adelaide on 29 June 1984. The maker is Ron Townsend. It is on DAT, 48 kHz/16-bit stereo, 1 hr 4 min. The contents are split into exterior and interior segments. They include 'chaff launcher loading and firing', missile loading, the 76 mm gun, the Harpoon launcher, Phalanx firing, engine room, wake, and a replenishment at sea with USS Taluga. No place is given. The page does not state mic placement, but other tapes in the same DPR series are described as 'close perspective'.
- **Fit:** This is the closest naval match I found in the AWM catalogue. It is a tube launcher on a warship's deck firing a round from close by, recorded at a real event by a service recordist. The same series also holds the 'Mortar - operational sounds' tape that the first search found. It is useful if a shipboard, deck-level launch thump is wanted rather than a land mortar.
- **Concerns:** A chaff launcher is not a depth-charge projector. Some chaff rounds are rocket-boosted, so there may be a whoosh after the launch thump, and the page does not say which round was fired. The chaff takes are inside a 64-minute tape, and the page gives no track timings. The recording is from the 1980s, not WW2. CC BY-NC is fine only while the game stays non-commercial.
- **Skeptic:** I read the page. It is S04691, 'HMAS Adelaide, Adelaide class guided missile Frigate - operational sounds', made by Defence Public Relations and Ron Townsend on 29 June 1984. It is on DAT (48 kHz, 16-bit, stereo) and runs 1 hr 4 min. The contents list 'Chaff launcher - firing' under the exterior top bridge area midships. 'Close perspective' appears in entries on this tape itself, not only in other tapes of the series. The page says © Australian War Memorial, CC BY-NC 3.0 AU, 'Only personal, non-commercial, research and study use permitted', which is acceptable while the game stays non-commercial. No place is given and there are no track timings. The page does not show whether the audio can be heard online or must be ordered. The fit is moderate. A chaff launcher (Mk 36 SRBOC type) is not a depth-charge projector, the round type is not stated, and a rocket-boosted round would add a whoosh after the thump. It is still the only naval tube-launcher firing found on the AWM site. No Limbo, depth-charge or destroyer-escort tape turned up.

The original camera owner is unknown, so there is no route to an acceptable licence from this page.

It is a small submersible imploding at about 3,300 m, picked up by a distant passive acoustic recorder, and such releases are often filtered or speed-shifted to be audible. It records a fatal accident in which five people died, so it is unsuitable as a game effect without the user's explicit choice. Rejected on fit and ethics, not on a hard rule.
