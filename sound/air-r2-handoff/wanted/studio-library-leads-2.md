## Torpedo launch and torpedo running (torpedo_launch, torpedo_run_loop)

### HMAS Oxley, Oberon class submarine - operational sounds (segment 24: "Torpedo Room - Firing torpedo")
- **For:** `torpedo_launch`. **URL:** https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C1240481
- **Licence:** CC BY-NC (3.0 AU) (skeptic: yes; page confirmed). The page says "Item copyright: © Australian War Memorial" and "This item is licensed under CC BY-NC", with a CC badge linking to CC BY-NC 3.0 AU. It also says "Only personal, non-commercial, research and study use permitted".
- **What it is:** A real recording made aboard the RAN Oberon-class submarine HMAS Oxley, about 1980, by Defence Public Relations (DPR). The original is a DAT tape (48 kHz, 16-bit, stereo) lasting 1 hr 16 min. It has 24 listed segments: ward room, bridge, periscope depth, klaxon alarms, sonar, an underwater cavitating propeller, and as segment 24, "Torpedo Room - Firing torpedo". The page has a single MP3 of the whole tape: https://s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/awm-media/collection/S04697/audio/3849992.MP3. Recording location and microphone placement are not stated.
- **Fit:** This is the only lead found that is a real torpedo tube firing on a real submarine. The Oberon class used 21-inch tubes, close to WW2 submarine practice. It would give the tube's discharge and air impulse from inside the boat.
- **Concerns:** It is heard from inside the torpedo room, so there is no outside splash. Its timecode inside the 76-minute MP3 is not given, but it is listed last, so it is probably near the end. The access copy is an MP3. The AWM labels the object type "Sound Effect", although it is a DPR field recording from a real boat; check that nothing was edited or layered. There may be crew talk or orders around the shot. The licence is non-commercial, which suits a personal game.
- **Skeptic:** torpedo_run_loop, segment 10. Confirmed on the page: under "Exterior" it lists "Underwater sound of cavitating propeller", after five "Operations room - underwater sonar sound" segments. The licence is CC BY-NC 3.0 AU, as above. The page does not say whose propeller it is. It may be Oxley's own, or a surface ship heard on the sonar. The same quality problem applies: the only online copy is a 22.05 kHz mono VBR MP3 averaging about 30 kbps. That matters less for a low-pass underwater texture, but it is still a lossy, low-bitrate source. The timecode is unknown. It is a large, slow propeller, so the pitch would need raising for a torpedo.

### Wolfset: underwater recordings of an electric RC ship-model motor and small Mercury outboards (anechoic tank, Lisbon Naval Base)
- **For:** `torpedo_run_loop`. **URL:** https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.25791978
- **Licence:** unknown for the data (the article is CC BY 4.0) (skeptic: unknown; page not confirmed). The article says "This article is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License", but no licence for the dataset itself is stated in the article or on the Springer Nature community page. The Figshare record could not be read from here (rate-limited, then a client error).
- **What it is:** About 5 hours of audio in 168 WAV files, recorded with a Brüel & Kjær 8104 hydrophone 2.5 m deep at the centre of an 8 x 5 x 5 m anechoic tank at Lisbon Naval Base. Signal chain: B&K 2636 amplifier, 22.4 kHz low-pass filter, 44.1 kHz 16-bit. Motors 1 to 4 are Mercury outboards of 4.5, 18, 8 and 3.6 hp, recorded at idle, slow, medium and varying throttle (about 45 to 57 minutes each). Motor 5 is the electric motor of a remote-controlled ship model with a 3-blade propeller, recorded at slow and fast forward and reverse, about 7 minutes in total. File names encode the motor settings, deliberate transients and noise (e.g. A01020T00N05.WAV), and an index is provided as Wolfset_Index.csv/xlsx.
- **Fit:** The best match found for a small electric motor and propeller heard on a calibrated hydrophone, the way an electric torpedo (such as the WW2 G7e) is heard. The steady single-motor files should loop well.
- **Concerns:** The licence must be confirmed on Figshare before use. Some files deliberately include transients (metal-tube impacts, air-rifle shots) and noise (bubbling, hoses, bucket pours), so pick files with codes T00 and N00. It is a tank, not open water. How the RC model was mounted is not described.
- **Skeptic:** torpedo_run_loop. My fetch budget ran out before I could re-read the Nature Scientific Data article or Figshare, so I cannot confirm that the dataset or its details exist. Kept only because the searcher reports reading the article, Figshare refused the searcher (effectively a blocked site), and an electric RC-model motor and propeller on a calibrated hydrophone is by far the closest match to an electric torpedo. It is a real recording in a tank, not a designed sound. The owner must open the Figshare record and confirm its licence (CC0 or CC BY would qualify) before any use. Use only files with no deliberate transients or noise. Motor 5 (the RC model) is only about 7 minutes in total.
